## 2. Schedule and Class Format

- **Organized by:** Institute for General Education, Sejong (department listed in the syllabus: Big Data Science)
- **Type:** Core liberal arts
- **Credits (Hours):** 3 (3)
- **Intended For:** Preferably second-year students and above
- **Class Times:** Tuesday periods 1-2, Wednesday period 3
- **Classrooms:** Nongshim International Building, Room 208 (Computer Lab 2) (Tue) and Public Policy Building, Room 220 (Wed)
- **Class Format:** Lectures, lab exercises, and Q&A.
  - Lectures and Q&A: Lectures center on the designated textbook, and active questions from students are welcome.
  - Lab exercises: Designed so that students practice R step by step from the basics and become able to clean, process, and analyze data and draw graphs.
- **Analysis Tool:** Statistics is the discipline of deriving information from data, so the course uses R, an open-source software program and a representative tool for data analysis.
- **Designated Books:** Excerpts from the three designated books in [Section 6](#6-references) are read together in class.
- **Attendance:** Attendance is checked (the course is not exempt from attendance checks).
- **Exam Proctoring:** Exams are not held without proctoring.
- **Course Description:** R is a highly suitable and effective tool for statistical analysis, graphing, and statistical programming. The course is for students new to statistics and, alongside slow, step-by-step R exercises, has them actually summarize data and draw graphs.
- **Learning Goals:** This is the age of data, in which large amounts of data in many forms are generated and stored in many fields. The course uses R, an open-source programming language, to process and interpret such data. For students new to R and to data analysis, it teaches how to clean and process data and draw graphs, and, if time allows, how to graph time series data and spatial data.
- **Core Competencies:** The course is very closely related to foresight, expertise, creativity, and global communication, and it cultivates these four competencies.
- **Prerequisites:** There is no recommended prerequisite course or enrollment requirement.

## 3. Assessment

| Item | Weight |
|:-----|-----:|
| Assignments | 10% |
| Midterm Exam | 35% |
| Final Exam | 40% |
| Participation | 15% |

- Two assignments are given in total, both as reports.
- The midterm and final exams consist mostly of multiple-choice and short-answer questions, with no long essay questions. They include multiple-choice and short-answer questions on the R programs covered in the textbook.
- Exams are held in person, and a missed exam cannot be replaced.
- **Report 1:** A report summarizing an interesting paper or report (preferably published in 2015 or later) found through the Korea University Library (library.korea.ac.kr). How to search for papers was explained in class on 9/23 (Tue).
  - In at least 4 pages, students write (1) a summary of the data, the statistical analysis methods, and the results of the analysis, and (2) their own opinion (impressions) of the paper and additional research beyond the chosen paper. The opinion and additional research must take at least 1 page.
  - The font size is 11. Figures may be copied as they are, but capturing the body text and inserting it as an image is penalized.
  - Papers that contain only multiple linear regression, analysis of variance, or frequency analysis are not allowed. Multiple regression is excluded because it is too easy, and other kinds of regression analysis may be used.
  - The final file is submitted to Blackboard as a PDF named `Global_StudentID_Name.pdf`, and the referenced paper as a PDF named `Global_StudentID_Name_Ref.pdf`.
  - The deadline was 10/4 (Sat) 18:00 and was extended to 10/7 (Tue) 18:00.
- **Report 2:** A report in which students choose at least 5 figures from each of Chapter 5 Bivariate Graphs, Chapter 6 Multivariate Graphs, Chapter 7 Maps, and Chapter 8 Time-dependent graphs of R. Kabacoff's *Modern Data Visualization with R* ([Section 6](#6-references)), at least 20 in total, type or copy and run the R code, and check the figures in the plot pane.
  - Students capture the code so that the colors of the R console pane are visible, and capture the figures in the plot pane.
  - After careful editing in hwp or Word so that the table of contents and each section can be told apart, the report is converted to PDF and submitted to the LMS as one file per student (`Global_StudentID_Name.pdf`).
  - The deadline is 11/29 (Sat) 18:00.
  - Students could ask the instructor about program errors by e-mail. E-mails received before 18:00 on weekdays were answered on the same day where possible, e-mails received on weekends and holidays were answered during the week, and only e-mails received by 11/27 (Thu) 18:00 were answered.
- **Assignment Grading Criteria:** The following deductions applied to both reports.
  - A missing submission is -10 points, and a late submission follows the late submission rule in [Section 4](#4-course-policies).
  - A file not in PDF format is -0.5 points, an inappropriate file name is -0.5 points, and each part that is hard for the grader to tell apart is -0.5 points.
  - A report that is impossible to grade because the text is too small or the editing is odd is -2 points, and copying and pasting without summarizing and without minimal editing is -1 point.
  - Copying generative AI output as is costs -3 points, copying someone else's report -10 points, and resubmitting a past assignment as is -10 points.
- **Report 1 Length and Content Deductions:** Falling short of the 4-page minimum is -3 points, not submitting the referenced paper -1 point, and a paper without data -1 point. A partly insufficient explanation of the data summary, statistical methods, and results is -1 point, and an overall very insufficient one -3 points. An opinion of half a page or less is -1 point, and no additional research beyond the paper -1 point. A paper that contains only multiple linear regression, analysis of variance, or frequency analysis, or a paper without the designated analyses, is -3 points each.
- **Report 2 Format and Content Deductions:** An odd chapter format is -1 point. Falling short of 5 figures in any of Chapters 5-8 is -0.5 points per missing figure, and a missing capture of either the R console pane or the plot pane is -0.5 points (up to 5 points).

## 4. Course Policies

- **Late Submission:** A late assignment receives 80% of its score after 1 day past the deadline, 60% after 2 days, 40% after 3 days, and 20% after 4 days.
- Submitting an assignment on scrap paper or torn-out notebook pages is penalized.
- Copying someone else's assignment or report gives both students 0 points, and resubmitting someone's past assignment as is also gives 0 points.
- Reports must be edited carefully so that the grader can tell each requirement apart, or points may be deducted.
- **Attendance:** Two late arrivals count as one absence, and arriving after half of the class time has passed counts as an absence.
- Missing one third or more of all classes results in an F.
- Unavoidable excused absences (death of an immediate family member, own hospitalization, reserve forces training) must be reported in advance.
- Other excused absences (menstrual leave, illness without hospitalization) and absences for department events are accepted only once.
- Individual classroom behavior such as sleeping, phone use, or leaving during class is prohibited.
- No exceptions are made, to keep assessment fair.
- **Academic Integrity:** Students have the following general duties in every part of completing the curriculum, including attending lectures, preparing and submitting assignments, and taking exams.
  1. Participate actively in all learning with a sincere, honest mind and a passionate attitude, in line with the purpose of the university and its educational philosophy.
  2. Be aware of their position as educated persons receiving higher education, and devote themselves to creative scholarly inquiry based on moral sensitivity.
  3. Faithfully keep the promises made to faculty or fellow students in connection with educational activities.
  4. Respect the dignity of faculty and fellow students and observe basic courtesy.
- Credits found to have been obtained through dishonest acts such as plagiarized assignments, reuse of assignments from other courses, or cheating on exams, or by other improper means, are revoked even after they have been granted, and disciplinary action such as reprimand, suspension, expulsion, or dismissal may follow, taking the circumstances of the act into account.
- The university runs a plagiarism detection system (SafeAssign on Blackboard) to raise awareness of academic integrity and foster creativity, and students must check in advance whether an assignment counts as plagiarism before submitting it.
